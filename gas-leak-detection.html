Gas leak detection service: safety steps, then a scheduled visit

Gas leaks are a safety issue first and a repair issue second. This page covers what to do in the moment, then explains how our licensed gas leak technicians can help once the immediate danger has passed.

Common warning signs of a gas leak

  • A distinct rotten-egg or sulfur smell, which utilities add to natural gas specifically so it's noticeable.
  • A hissing or whistling sound near a gas line, meter, or appliance.
  • Dead or discolored vegetation in an otherwise healthy area near a buried gas line.
  • Dirt or dust being blown into the air near a gas line for no clear reason.
  • Physical symptoms in occupants such as dizziness, nausea, or headaches that ease when away from the building.

What NOT to do if you suspect a gas leak

  • Don't light a match, candle, or lighter to "check."
  • Don't flip light switches or unplug appliances on your way out.
  • Don't use a landline or cell phone inside the building — wait until you're outside and at a safe distance.
  • Don't try to locate or stop the leak yourself.
  • Don't go back inside until a utility or emergency responder confirms it's safe.

Gas leak FAQs

Should I call you or 911 first?

If you suspect an active gas leak, leave the area and contact your gas utility's emergency line or 911 first. Contact us afterward, once the situation has been addressed, to arrange inspection or repair.

Are your technicians licensed?

Yes. Our gas leak technicians hold current state licensing and insurance. We're happy to provide license and insurance details on request before any work begins.

What if it turns out to be a false alarm?

That's fine, and it's always better to check. A qualified technician can confirm there's no leak and check that appliances and connections are in good condition.
